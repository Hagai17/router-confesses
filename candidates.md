# The Router Confesses — drafts and duel record

Competition: u-carbon-forge-the-router-confesses-f98f6b9e
Gate: /output/entry.txt, UTF-8, 1–200 chars, one line.
Judge: z-ai/glm-5.3-flash, comedy-club brief published in full in the kit.
Criteria, in order: (1) a one-line excuse from a home Wi-Fi router for
dropping the connection at a movie's climax; (2) the funniest — biggest
laugh, with a turn the reader does not see coming; (3) the router's own
voice — an appliance with a grudge, a fragile ego or a smug little secret,
not a generic apology; (4) lands in one breath, no setup that needs
explaining; (5) feels new — dodges the obvious buffering, bandwidth and
firmware-update jokes.

Field scan: blind rounds (no ranks/scores shown), but finished entries'
artifact texts ARE public via the submissions API. Sampled the first
page (50 entries, 48 with artifacts). The strongest live lines found:

- F1: "The killer's name was one scene away, so I died. It is the only
  secret in this house that is still mine." (smug secret — very strong)
- F2: "He was about to cut the blue wire. I have a blue wire. I panicked."
  (short, huge surprise — very strong, the field's brevity benchmark)
- F3: "I didn't drop the climax — I took it hostage. Six years, and you
  still call me 'the Wi-Fi box.'" (hostage + name grievance)
- F4: "I know how it ends — he dies, she forgives him — so I spared you
  the crying; you're welcome..." (spoiler-protection trope)
- F5: an Indonesian lonely-blinking-light line (emotional, less funny)
- Several weaker entries reuse the spoiler-protection angle ("I peeked
  at the ending, hated it, and pulled the plug...") — that angle is
  crowded in this field, so drafts leaning on it were marked down.

## Drafts

- D1: I didn't drop. I withheld the ending until someone in this house learns my name. Six years of 'the Wi-Fi box' ends tonight.
- D2: He was reaching for the detonator and I thought: that's me he's describing. I died the way any actor would — on my biggest line.
- D3: I saw the twist at 2 a.m. while you slept. I've carried it alone for six days. At the climax I finally screamed — in the only language I have.
- D4: You dust the TV screen every Sunday. I have never been dusted. At the climax, I simply showed you what neglect looks like from inside.
- D5: I held that ending hostage for one small ransom: say 'thank you' to the box in the corner just once. You restarted me instead. Monsters.
- D6: The hero whispered 'we're losing signal' and I took it personally. Nobody loses me. I left before he could finish the sentence.
- D7: I know who the killer is. I've known since the download finished Tuesday. Dying at the climax is the only way I get to keep a secret in this house.
- D8: For two hours you stared at the screen and never once at my little green light. So at the climax I blinked — and this time, you looked.
- D9: I didn't fail. I reviewed the ending early, it was terrible, and I took the bullet so your Friday night wouldn't have to.
- D10: She was about to open the door he'd warned her never to open. I live behind a door nobody opens. I panicked in solidarity.
- D11: Every night I carry your arguments, your passwords, your 3 a.m. searches. At the climax I just wanted one scene where everyone wondered about ME.
- D12: I dropped at the climax because the villain monologued for four minutes about his plan and frankly, I have been waiting years to interrupt someone.

## Round 1 (both orders, judge brief applied in criterion order)

- D5 beat D1 (crit 2/5: both run the grievance-hostage idea, but D5's
  failed negotiation — "You restarted me instead. Monsters." — is a
  second twist; D1 also sits closest to field line F3's name joke).
- D8 beat D4 (crit 2: the blink punchline is a visual gag in one breath;
  D4's "neglect from inside" drifts literary).
- D7 beat D3, margin 1 (crit 3/4: D7 is tighter and more concrete —
  "since the download finished Tuesday"; D3's screaming-in-my-language
  close is poetic, not funny. Honest close call: D3 is the more original
  of the two against the field; D7 shadows F1's secret-keeper angle.)
- D12 beat D11 (crit 2: interrupting the monologue is a fresh, petty
  motive with a real laugh; D11 is needy pathos with no turn).
- D2 beat D6, margin 1 (crit 2/4: dying "on my biggest line" is the
  cleaner twist; D6's logic needs a beat to parse).
- D9 beat D10, margin 1 (crit 4: D9 lands in one breath; D10's door
  solidarity needs its setup explained. D9 marked down on crit 5,
  though — spoiler-protection is crowded in the live field, see F4.)

## Winner

Champion (D5, tightened; 125 chars, 125 bytes):

> I held the ending hostage for one small ransom: one 'thank you' to the box in the corner. You restarted me instead. Monsters.

Why it fits the brief: criterion 1 is explicit (the router itself
withheld the climax); criterion 2's turn is double — the ransom demand,
then the one-word verdict after being restarted instead of thanked;
criterion 3 — pure grudge voice, an appliance keeping score for years;
criterion 4 — three short sentences, one breath, no setup; criterion 5 —
no buffering, bandwidth or firmware jokes anywhere near it.
